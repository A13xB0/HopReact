Why some cells say “—”. Only adverts carry their sender's identity. Every other kind of packet is encrypted and identifies its sender with a single byte, which on this mesh narrows it to about three nodes — not one. So we can tell you what a node passed on, and what it advertised, but not what it sent.

And why counts can look low. A node is only identifiable in a route when that route uses hashes of three bytes or more, which is roughly four packets in ten. A rule with no packets behind it stays quiet instead of treating missing evidence as silence.
